Where the day goes.

Three numbers from 2026 law-firm reports. The sources are at the bottom of the page.

A small office doesn’t lose the day on the clinical question or the legal question. It loses it on intake, chasing records, recalls, and follow-ups. A home spends the same kind of time on the calendar, the mail, and finding a file. Three 2026 reports looked at small law firms and landed on that admin. Dental practices and clinics describe the same pile. The counts below are the law-firm surveys, and that is where they stay.

  • 4 days

    Of 304 US lawyers ShareFile surveyed, about half in-house and half at firms, 47% say client intake takes four days or longer. Most think it should take two or less.1

  • 5 hours

    89% of the small-firm legal professionals AI Lawyer surveyed lose five or more hours a week to non-billable admin. Intake is the stage they name most often for errors.2

  • 33%

    For a third of small firms in Clio’s survey, finding the time is the biggest hurdle to new technology. There is no IT desk to set it up.3

A lot of those firms already pay for AI. Clio found that just under a third of the firms using it had increased revenue. Someone still has to set the tool up and keep it running. If the tool is ChatGPT, Anthropic, or an Apple, Google, or Alexa hub, the prompts and any records people paste in sit on that company’s servers. You pay per seat or per token for as long as you use it. Their terms are the terms. A computer you own, in the office or a closet, is the other arrangement. We install an open-weight model on it. The answer is computed there. The prompt is not sent to OpenAI, Anthropic, or Apple’s servers to get it.

What the box does.

Five jobs. The Never column is the limit.

The same five jobs cover a dental office, a small firm, and a house. Each one has a line the local model doesn’t cross, and a person who signs off. We write both down before anything is connected, and both show up in the portal.

Why it stays in the building.

Written down before we connect anything. This page isn’t the contract.

A law firm has to be able to put this in a vendor file. Many use ABA Formal Opinion 512, with Rule 1.6 on confidentiality and Rule 5.3 on supervising a nonlawyer who touches the work.5 A dental practice keeps its own HIPAA safeguards. We answer those questions in writing before the work starts.

  • The model runs on a computer you own, in the office or the house. No outside AI company holds the prompts, the patient files, the client files, or the household files. There is no usage log of those prompts outside your walls. Files on the machine are not used to train a public model.
  • Owning the computer takes the outside AI company out of the picture. It does not, by itself, make a practice HIPAA compliant. Your access controls and office policies still apply. A dentist does the clinical work. A lawyer does the legal work. The model does not diagnose, recommend treatment, or replace that judgment.
  • We do not copy patient or client files onto our servers. The portal keeps the record of what ran, including drafts and who approved them. A draft can contain that information. The record is yours, and you can have it deleted.
  • The model is an open-weight model on your machine. Models are swappable. We test a new one on your actual jobs before it replaces a model that works. If you stop working with us, the computer stays on your shelf, with the model on it.
  • If the internet or a cloud provider goes down, search and drafting on the local model still run. A message that has to be sent waits until the connection is back.
  • Access is the least the job needs. It is written down before anything is connected, you can revoke it, and a confidentiality agreement is signed first. You can stop the jobs. The portal shows what ran, what is waiting, and what was blocked.
  • We do not have a SOC 2 report. If your vendor file needs one, we will say so in writing, with who can see what.

How the group works.

Finding clients is the hard part, so we do it together. When you join, Roger sends you an API key for the shared mailbox. Each engineer runs their own Grok Bot and agents, emailing and posting on X to bring in leads, all under the Algorithmacy Studio name.

You run your own shop. A client who comes in through your outreach is your client. When you have as many as you can handle, say 50, you send new ones to other engineers in the group to manage. That is how it grows.

One engineer’s agents only reach so far. Everyone’s agents, coordinated under one brand, reach a lot further. That is the reason for the group.
